BLS / CPR

Skill I — Adult BLS / CPR
  1. Ensure scene safety — check surroundings for hazards
  2. Check responsiveness — tap shoulders, shout "Are you okay?"
  3. Shout for help; direct someone to call 911 and get AED
  4. Check for breathing and carotid pulse (no more than 10 seconds)
  5. If no pulse: begin CPR — 30 compressions : 2 breaths
  6. Compression depth: at least 5 cm; rate: 100–120 per minute
  7. Allow full chest recoil between compressions
  8. Head tilt–chin lift to open airway; give 2 rescue breaths (1 sec each, visible chest rise)
  9. Continue until AED arrives, trained rescuer takes over, or victim recovers
Skill II — Adult BLS with AED
  1. Follow all steps of Adult BLS / CPR (Skill I)
  2. Power on AED as soon as it arrives — do NOT stop CPR until AED is ready
  3. Attach pads: one below right collarbone, one below left armpit
  4. Plug in connector and let AED analyze rhythm — stand clear, no touching
  5. If shock advised: ensure no one is touching patient, press shock button
  6. Immediately resume CPR for 2 minutes after shock
  7. If no shock advised: continue CPR and follow AED prompts
Skill III — Infant BLS / CPR
  1. Check scene safety; check responsiveness (flick foot sole)
  2. Send someone to call 911; if alone, give 5 cycles of CPR first
  3. Check brachial or femoral pulse (no more than 10 seconds)
  4. If no pulse: place 2 fingers (middle and ring) just below the nipple line on the sternum
  5. Compress at least 4 cm deep; rate 100–120 per minute
  6. 30:2 ratio (1 rescuer) or 15:2 (2 rescuers)
  7. Neutral head position to open airway; give gentle puffs (watch for chest rise)
Skill IV — Severe Bleeding & Shock
  1. Don gloves (PPE) before touching blood or bodily fluids
  2. Apply dressings over the wound; press firmly with heel of hand
  3. Stack second hand on top — maintain constant, firm pressure
  4. Do NOT remove dressings — add more on top if soaking through
  5. If limb: apply tourniquet 5–8 cm above wound (not on a joint); note time
  6. Watch for shock signs: pale/cool/clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, confusion
  7. Have victim lie down, keep warm, call 911
Skill V — Choking (Adult / Child)
  1. Confirm severe airway block: unable to speak, cough, or breathe
  2. Ask "Are you choking? Can I help?" — if yes, say "I'm going to help you"
  3. Stand or kneel firmly behind the victim
  4. Make a fist — place thumb side just above navel, well below breastbone
  5. Grasp fist with other hand; give quick, firm upward thrusts
  6. Continue until object is expelled or victim becomes unresponsive
  7. If unresponsive: lower to floor, begin CPR; look in mouth before each breath
Skill VI — Open Fracture & Splinting
  1. Call 911; keep victim still — do NOT try to straighten the bone
  2. Cover any open wound / protruding bone with a clean dressing
  3. Immobilize using a splint extending beyond the joints above and below the fracture
  4. Pad the splint for comfort; secure with bandages — not too tight
  5. Check circulation, sensation, and movement below the injury before and after splinting
  6. Elevate the limb if possible to reduce swelling
  7. Apply ice wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling (not directly on skin)
Skill VII — Severe Burns
  1. Ensure safety; if person is on fire: Stop, Drop, and Roll
  2. Cover with a wet blanket to extinguish flames
  3. Call 911 for large or severe burns
  4. Cool the burn immediately with cold (not ice-cold) running water for at least 10 minutes
  5. Remove jewelry and clothing that is NOT stuck to the skin
  6. Cover with dry, non-stick, sterile dressings
  7. Cover victim with dry blanket; monitor for shock
Skill VIII — Heat-Related Emergency
  1. Move victim to a cool place; call 911 if heat stroke suspected
  2. Remove excess clothing
  3. Cool immediately: cool water spray, cool damp cloths on forehead, neck, armpits, groin
  4. Immerse in cool water up to neck if available (for heat stroke)
  5. If responsive and able to swallow: give fluids with sugar and electrolytes
  6. Stop cooling when victim behaves normally (to prevent hypothermia)
  7. If unresponsive and not breathing normally: begin CPR
Skill IX — Administer Epinephrine Pen
  1. Confirm signs of anaphylaxis: hives, swelling, difficulty breathing, low BP
  2. Call 911 immediately
  3. Remove epinephrine pen from case; remove safety cap
  4. Grip firmly in dominant hand
  5. Press tip firmly against outer mid-thigh (can be given through clothing)
  6. Push down until a click is heard; hold in place for 10 seconds
  7. Remove, massage the area, note time of injection; dispose in sharps container
Skill X — Administer Naloxone (NARCAN)
  1. Recognize signs of opioid overdose: unresponsive, slow/absent breathing, blue lips
  2. Call 911; ensure scene is safe
  3. Check for breathing — if not breathing normally, begin CPR
  4. Peel back foil packaging; hold device with thumb on bottom, two fingers on nozzle
  5. Tilt head back; support neck; insert nozzle into one nostril
  6. Press thumb firmly to release full dose
  7. If no response after 2–3 minutes, give second dose in other nostril; continue CPR as needed

Skill I — Patient Assessment: Trauma
  1. Take body substance isolation precautions (BSI/PPE)
  2. Assess scene safety and mechanism of injury
  3. Determine number of patients; request additional resources if needed
  4. Perform general impression; identify chief complaint
  5. Assess responsiveness (AVPU scale)
  6. Immobilise C-spine if indicated
  7. Assess airway — open with jaw thrust if trauma suspected
  8. Assess breathing rate, depth, effort; apply O₂ or ventilate as needed
  9. Assess circulation: pulse, major bleeding, skin signs
  10. Determine transport priority; identify immediate life threats
  11. Perform rapid trauma assessment (head-to-toe)
  12. Obtain baseline vital signs
  13. Obtain SAMPLE history
  14. Call in patient report to hospital
Skill II — Patient Assessment: Medical
  1. BSI/PPE precautions
  2. Scene safety; general impression
  3. Assess responsiveness (AVPU)
  4. Airway and breathing assessment
  5. Circulation: pulse, perfusion, skin
  6. Determine transport priority
  7. Perform history of present illness (OPQRST)
  8. SAMPLE history
  9. Perform focused physical exam based on chief complaint
  10. Baseline vital signs
  11. Reassess and transmit patient report
Skill III — BVM Ventilation: Apneic Adult
  1. BSI; assess scene safety
  2. Confirm patient is apneic (unresponsive, no breathing)
  3. Position patient supine with head neutral
  4. Open airway — head tilt–chin lift or jaw thrust
  5. Select correctly sized mask; create seal over nose and mouth
  6. Attach BVM to 15 L/min O₂
  7. Deliver ventilation: squeeze bag over 1 second, watch for chest rise
  8. Rate: 10–12 breaths per minute for adults
  9. Continue ventilation; reassess after every 2 minutes
Skill IV — Joint / Long Bone Immobilisation
  1. BSI; assess scene and MOI
  2. Expose and assess injury (PMS: pulse, motor, sensation)
  3. Select appropriate splint (rigid, soft, traction)
  4. Stabilise joint above and below injury
  5. Apply padding over bony prominences
  6. Secure splint — snug but not tight (fit 2 fingers under straps)
  7. Reassess PMS after splinting
  8. Elevate extremity if appropriate
Skill V — Bleeding Control / Shock Management
  1. BSI — gloves mandatory before contact with blood
  2. Apply direct pressure with gauze pad — use full force
  3. If bleeding continues: pack wound firmly and maintain pressure
  4. Apply tourniquet if extremity bleeding is uncontrollable — note time applied
  5. Manage for shock: keep warm, supine, legs elevated (unless contraindicated)
  6. Administer high-flow O₂
  7. Transport rapidly
Skill VI — Cardiac Arrest Management / AED
  1. BSI; confirm cardiac arrest (unresponsive, apneic, no pulse)
  2. Begin CPR: 30 compressions : 2 breaths
  3. Power on AED as soon as available
  4. Attach pads; stop CPR for rhythm analysis
  5. Ensure no one touching patient during analysis
  6. If shock advised: clear patient, deliver shock
  7. Resume CPR immediately for 2 minutes
  8. Reassess every 2 minutes
Skill VII — O₂ by Non-Rebreather Mask
  1. Obtain order or confirm indication
  2. Select NRB mask and attach to O₂ tubing
  3. Open O₂ tank; set flow rate to 15 L/min
  4. Pre-fill reservoir bag before applying (occlude exhalation port)
  5. Place mask on patient — snug seal over nose and mouth
  6. Adjust straps; confirm reservoir bag inflates with inhalation
  7. Monitor respiratory rate and O₂ saturation
Skill VIII — Administer Epinephrine Auto-Injector
  1. Confirm anaphylaxis: hives, swelling, respiratory distress, hypotension
  2. Follow local protocol for authorisation
  3. Obtain patient's own auto-injector (or carry per protocol)
  4. Remove from carrier; remove safety cap
  5. Place tip against outer mid-thigh (may inject through clothing)
  6. Press firmly until click; hold for 10 seconds
  7. Remove; massage injection site for 10 seconds
  8. Note time; reassess; prepare for transport
Skill IX — Administer Naloxone (Nasal NARCAN)
  1. Confirm suspected opioid overdose: unresponsive, slow/absent respirations, pinpoint pupils
  2. Ensure airway; provide ventilation as needed
  3. Assemble nasal atomiser device
  4. Insert tip into one nostril; deliver half dose (1 mL)
  5. Insert into other nostril; deliver remaining half
  6. Continue ventilations; reassess for response in 2–3 min
  7. May repeat dose if no improvement after 2–3 min
  8. Transport; monitor for re-sedation

Skill I — Anatomical Landmark Identification (7 min)
  1. Identify bones and bony landmarks of the region specified
  2. Identify ligaments and relevant soft tissue structures
  3. Identify muscles by name including origin, insertion, belly, and tendons
  4. Regions covered: foot, ankle/lower leg, knee, elbow/forearm, wrist/hand/fingers, shoulder
Skill II — Joint Identification & Maximum Range of Motion (15 min)
  1. Identify the specified joint
  2. Demonstrate each plane of motion for that joint
  3. State the maximum normal range of motion in degrees for each plane
  4. Use correct anatomical terminology throughout
Skill III-A — Ankle Taping (Inversion) (5 min)
  1. Position ankle in neutral (90°) dorsiflexion
  2. Apply pre-wrap from metatarsal heads to mid-calf
  3. Apply 2 anchor strips at the top
  4. Apply 3 stirrups from anchor, under heel, to anchor on opposite side
  5. Apply horseshoe strips following stirrups
  6. Apply heel locks (lateral and medial)
  7. Close with 2–3 overlapping circular strips from anchors down
  8. Check circulation — ensure 2 fingers fit under tape
Skill III-B — Achilles Tendon Taping (6 min)
  1. Position patient prone with foot in slight plantar flexion
  2. Apply pre-wrap from metatarsal heads to mid-calf
  3. Apply 2 anchors at top
  4. Apply Achilles tendon strips — fan posteriorly over tendon
  5. Close with 2–3 circular strips
  6. Check fit and circulation
Skill III-C — Shoulder Spica Taping (5 min)
  1. Position arm in slight flexion and internal rotation
  2. Apply pre-wrap from shoulder to mid-bicep
  3. Apply anchors at top of shoulder
  4. Apply figure-eight strips wrapping around arm and across chest
  5. Close and ensure range of motion is appropriately limited
Skill III-D — Wrist/Hand, Circular Wrist / Figure Eight (4 min)
  1. Apply pre-wrap from hand to mid-forearm
  2. Apply circular wraps around the wrist for circular technique
  3. For figure-eight: wrap around wrist, across palm, and back around wrist alternating direction
  4. Ensure thumb is free and circulation is unimpaired

Skill I — Identifying Equipment (15 min)
  1. 15 pharmacy instruments/equipment photos laid out
  2. Identify name of each instrument or equipment piece
  3. State the purpose or use of each item
  4. Covers: syringes, needles, compounding tools, measuring devices, IV equipment, and dispensing tools
Skill II — Verifying Controlled Substance Prescription & DEA Number (6 min)
  1. Receive written prescription for a controlled substance
  2. Verify all required prescription elements are present: patient name/DOB, prescriber name/address/phone, drug name/strength/quantity/directions, date, manual signature
  3. Verify DEA number format: 2 letters + 7 digits (e.g. AB1234563)
  4. Validate DEA checksum: add digits 1+3+5; add 2×(digits 2+4+6); sum must match 7th digit
  5. Document findings on provided verification sheet
Skill III — Withdrawing Liquid from a Vial (15 min)
  1. Perform hand hygiene; don gloves
  2. Gather supplies: appropriate syringe, needle, alcohol swab, vial
  3. Inspect vial for particulates, expiry, and integrity
  4. Swab vial rubber stopper with alcohol; let dry
  5. Draw air into syringe equal to desired volume
  6. Insert needle bevel-up at 45°, then advance to 90°
  7. Inject air into vial (positive pressure); invert vial
  8. Withdraw correct volume; remove air bubbles
  9. Remove needle; recap using one-hand scoop method
Skill IV — Compounding an Oral Suspension (15 min)
  1. Calculate required quantity of drug and vehicle
  2. Gather ingredients: drug powder or tablets, suspending vehicle, graduated cylinder, mortar and pestle
  3. Triturate tablets to fine powder in mortar
  4. Add small amount of vehicle to create paste; mix thoroughly
  5. Transfer to calibrated bottle; rinse mortar with vehicle and add to bottle
  6. Add vehicle to final volume; cap and shake well
  7. Label with drug name, strength, quantity, instructions, expiry, storage, and "Shake Well" auxiliary label
Skill V — Patient Screening for Pharmacist Consultation (4 min)
  1. Greet patient professionally
  2. Confirm patient name and date of birth
  3. Review prescription for completeness and appropriateness
  4. Screen for allergies, current medications, and contraindications
  5. Identify patients who require mandatory pharmacist counselling (new Rx, controlled substances)
  6. Refer appropriately to pharmacist; document screening
Skill VI — Filling a Prescription (5 min)
  1. Receive and review prescription for completeness
  2. Enter prescription data accurately into system
  3. Perform drug utilisation review (DUR) — check interactions, allergies
  4. Select correct drug, strength, and dosage form from stock
  5. Count or measure correct quantity using counting tray or balance
  6. Label container accurately; affix required auxiliary labels
  7. Prepare for pharmacist final verification
Skill VII — Aseptic Garbing, Hand Washing & Gloving (10 min)
  1. Remove jewellery; tie back hair
  2. Don shoe covers, then hair cover/bouffant
  3. Don face mask — secure over nose and mouth
  4. Perform surgical hand wash: scrub hands, wrists, and forearms for minimum 30 seconds
  5. Rinse hands pointing downward; dry with sterile towels
  6. Don sterile gown without touching outside surface
  7. Don sterile gloves using closed gloving technique
  8. Move to clean room without contaminating gown or gloves

Skill I — Ambulating with a Transfer (Gait) Belt (5 min)
  1. Explain procedure to patient; obtain consent
  2. Apply gait belt snugly around patient's waist over clothing — fit 2 fingers underneath
  3. Position yourself beside and slightly behind patient
  4. Grasp belt from behind with dominant hand; support forearm with other hand
  5. Cue patient to stand; assist as needed
  6. Walk slightly behind patient; maintain grasp on belt at all times
  7. Observe gait; provide only needed assistance
  8. Return patient to chair safely; document
Skill II — Ambulating with a Walker (9 min)
  1. Ensure walker is correct height: top level with patient's wrist crease when arm is at side
  2. Place all 4 walker tips flat on floor — never use a tilted walker
  3. Pick up walker and advance it one step length ahead
  4. Step into walker with affected leg, then unaffected leg
  5. Maintain 3-point gait if partial weight bearing; 4-point if full weight bearing
  6. Instruct patient not to use walker as support when sitting or standing up
Skill III — Ambulating with a Cane (8 min)
  1. Cane height: handle level with wrist crease; 30° elbow flexion when grasping
  2. Hold cane in hand opposite to affected leg
  3. Advance cane and affected leg simultaneously (2-point gait) or advance cane first (3-point)
  4. Bring unaffected leg forward to or past cane
  5. Stairs UP: unaffected leg first, then cane + affected leg ("up with the good")
  6. Stairs DOWN: cane + affected leg first, then unaffected leg ("down with the bad")
Skill IV — Ambulating with Crutches (7 min)
  1. Axillary crutches: 2–3 finger widths between axilla and crutch pad; elbows at 30° flexion
  2. Weight must be on hands, NOT axilla (to prevent axillary nerve damage)
  3. Swing-to gait: advance both crutches, swing body to crutches
  4. 3-point gait (non-weight bearing): advance both crutches + affected leg; step through with unaffected leg
  5. Stairs UP: unaffected leg first, then crutches + affected leg
  6. Stairs DOWN: lower crutches + affected leg first, then unaffected leg
Skill V — Passive Range of Motion (10 min)
  1. Explain procedure; position patient comfortably
  2. Support the limb proximally and distally to the joint being moved
  3. Move joint through full available range — do not force movement
  4. Perform each motion smoothly and rhythmically
  5. Observe patient for pain; stop if resistance or discomfort
  6. Key motions: flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, internal/external rotation, circumduction where applicable
  7. Perform 3–5 repetitions per motion; document
Skill VI — Cold Application with Ice Bag (6 min)
  1. Check physician order and assess area; contraindications: open wounds, circulatory issues, Raynaud's
  2. Fill ice bag ⅔ full with small ice pieces; remove excess air; seal securely
  3. Wrap in towel or pillowcase (never apply ice directly to skin)
  4. Apply to affected area; secure with ties or patient holding
  5. Treat for 15–20 minutes maximum
  6. Assess skin every 5 minutes for numbness, colour change, or blistering
  7. Remove and document application time and patient response
Skill VII — Transfer: Supine to Sitting (5 min)
  1. Lower bed to lowest position; raise HOB to 30°
  2. Log roll patient to side-lying facing edge of bed
  3. Lower legs off edge of bed while pushing torso upright simultaneously
  4. Allow patient to pause at edge to regain balance
  5. Support patient during sitting phase
  6. Assess for orthostatic changes before standing
Skill VIII — Donning & Removing Isolation Garments (5 min)
  1. ORDER OF DONNING: Gown → Mask/Respirator → Goggles/Face shield → Gloves
  2. Gown: open at back, secure at neck and waist
  3. Mask: fit over nose and mouth; mould nose bar; check seal
  4. Goggles: fit snugly over eyes; no gaps
  5. Gloves: pull over gown cuffs
  6. ORDER OF REMOVAL (doff): Gloves → Goggles → Gown → Mask
  7. Remove gloves inside-out; goggles from behind; gown roll inward; mask by strings — never touch front
  8. Hand hygiene after each removal step

Skill I — Identifying Lab Instruments/Equipment (15 min)
  1. 15 instruments are laid out; identify each by name
  2. State the purpose of each instrument
  3. Potential instruments include: microscopes, centrifuges, spectrophotometers, pipettes, haematocytometers, incubators, autoclave, haemacytometer, coagulation analysers, and more
Skill II — Infection Control & Transmission-Based Precautions (5 min)
  1. Identify the type of transmission precaution required for the given scenario (Contact, Droplet, Airborne)
  2. Select and don appropriate PPE in correct order
  3. Demonstrate correct hand hygiene technique
  4. Doff PPE in correct order without self-contamination
  5. Dispose of PPE in appropriate waste containers
Skill III — Using a Microscope (10 min)
  1. Clean ocular and objective lenses with lens paper
  2. Place slide on stage; secure with stage clips
  3. Start on lowest power objective (4×)
  4. Use coarse focus knob to bring specimen into view
  5. Switch to 10× then 40× — use fine focus only on higher powers
  6. Adjust condenser and diaphragm for optimal illumination
  7. Record findings; clean and return to low power before removing slide
Skill IV — ABO Grouping (6 min)
  1. Label slide or card with patient ID
  2. Place one drop of patient red blood cells in each of 2 wells
  3. Add anti-A reagent to first well; anti-B to second
  4. Mix with separate applicator sticks
  5. Rock card gently for 2 minutes
  6. Observe for agglutination (clumping = positive reaction)
  7. Interpret: agglutination with anti-A = type A; anti-B = type B; both = type AB; neither = type O
Skill V-A — Preparing a Blood Film/Smear (5 min)
  1. Place small drop of blood (3 mm) near frosted end of clean slide
  2. Hold spreader slide at 30–45° angle; back into drop until it spreads across width
  3. Push forward in one smooth motion to create feathered edge
  4. Air dry completely — do not heat or blow
  5. Label with patient name and date on frosted end
Skill V-B — Staining a Blood Film/Smear (10 min)
  1. Fix air-dried smear with methanol for 30–60 seconds
  2. Flood slide with Wright's stain; wait 3–5 minutes
  3. Add equal volume of buffer; mix gently by blowing; wait 5 minutes
  4. Rinse with distilled water; drain and air dry
  5. Examine under microscope — WBCs should show purple granules; RBCs pink; platelets purple
Skill VI — Physical Examination of Urine (6 min)
  1. Don PPE
  2. Assess colour (pale yellow to amber — note abnormal colours)
  3. Assess clarity (clear, hazy, cloudy, turbid)
  4. Measure specific gravity with refractometer or urinometer
  5. Record all findings
Skill VII — Inoculate and Streak Agar Plate (5 min)
  1. Label plate bottom with patient ID, date, media type
  2. Using inoculating loop or swab, streak specimen across ¼ of plate (Zone 1)
  3. Flame loop; rotate plate 90°; streak through Zone 1 into Zone 2
  4. Flame loop; rotate 90°; streak through Zone 2 into Zone 3
  5. Flame loop; rotate 90°; streak through Zone 3 into Zone 4 (isolated colonies expected here)
  6. Invert plate; incubate at 35–37°C for 18–24 hours

Skill I — Dental Instrument Identification (15 min)
  1. Name and state the purpose of 15 instruments from the comprehensive list
  2. Key instruments include: amalgam carrier, condenser/plugger, amalgam well, aspirating syringe, explorer, mirror, spoon excavator, burnisher, carver, matrix band/retainer, tofflemire retainer, rubber dam clamp, forceps, elevator, curette
Skill II — Assembly/Disassembly of Aspirating Anesthetic Syringe (7 min)
  1. Wash hands; don gloves and mask
  2. Hold syringe with thumb ring facing down
  3. Retract piston fully; insert cartridge (narrow end toward needle end)
  4. Advance piston — engage harpoon into rubber end of cartridge with firm push
  5. Attach needle using needle adapter (never recap with two hands)
  6. Test by aspirating: slight blood return confirms intravenous placement
  7. Disassemble in reverse: cap needle; remove cartridge; retract harpoon; dispose of needle in sharps container
Skill III — Seat, Prepare & Dismiss Patient for Oral Evaluation (10 min)
  1. Greet patient; escort to dental chair
  2. Seat patient upright; adjust headrest
  3. Place patient napkin/bib with napkin clip
  4. Update/review medical history; confirm allergies
  5. Adjust dental light for illumination without shining in patient's eyes
  6. Position operator stool and assistant stool appropriately
  7. Following examination: remove all drapes; return chair to upright position
  8. Escort patient to reception; document
Skill IV — Placing & Removing Surface Barriers (7 min)
  1. Identify all items requiring surface barriers (light handles, chair controls, X-ray heads, computer mouse, bracket tray)
  2. Don gloves; apply barriers over all identified surfaces
  3. Ensure complete coverage with no gaps
  4. Following patient contact: remove barriers without touching underlying surface
  5. Remove gloves; wash hands
  6. Apply fresh barriers before next patient
Skill V — Oral Hygiene Instruction: Brushing & Flossing (7 min)
  1. Explain importance of oral hygiene to patient at appropriate level
  2. Select correct soft-bristle toothbrush
  3. Demonstrate Bass technique: 45° to gum line, small circular motions, 2 teeth at a time
  4. Brush all surfaces: buccal, lingual, occlusal, and inner surfaces
  5. Brush tongue
  6. Demonstrate proper flossing: 45 cm, wrap around middle fingers, use thumbs and forefingers to guide
  7. Curve floss into C-shape around tooth; slide under gumline gently
  8. Use clean section of floss for each tooth
Skill VI — Alginate Impressions: Preparation, Manipulation, Delivery & Handling (7 min)
  1. Select correct tray size by trial fitting in patient's mouth
  2. Measure water and alginate powder per manufacturer ratio
  3. Add powder to water; mix vigorously with spatula for 45–60 seconds until smooth
  4. Load tray: fill fully, smooth surface, no voids
  5. Seat tray in mouth; instruct patient to breathe through nose
  6. Hold stable for 3–5 minutes until set (no elasticity when touched)
  7. Remove with firm snap motion; rinse impression immediately
  8. Pour in stone within 30 minutes or wrap in damp gauze
Skill VII — Operating the Ultrasonic Cleaner (5 min)
  1. Don utility gloves, mask, eyewear, and gown
  2. Fill cleaner with appropriate enzymatic solution to fill line
  3. Place instruments in basket — do not overfill; do not hand-sort dirty instruments

Skill II — Restriction Digestion Reaction (15 min)
  1. Keep all reagents on ice until needed
  2. Label microcentrifuge tube with sample ID
  3. Add reagents in correct order: water → buffer → DNA → enzyme (enzyme last)
  4. Pipette each component accurately per protocol volume
  5. Mix by gentle vortex or flicking; briefly centrifuge to collect
  6. Incubate at 37°C for specified time (typically 60 min)
  7. Heat inactivate at 65°C for 20 min or add loading dye and store on ice

  1. Prepare 1% agarose gel: dissolve in TAE buffer by microwaving
  2. Cool to ~55°C; add ethidium bromide or GelRed if required
  3. Pour into gel tray with comb; allow to solidify (30 min)
  4. Remove comb; place gel in electrophoresis chamber
  5. Cover with TAE buffer just above gel surface
  6. Load 5–15 µL samples into wells; include DNA ladder in first well
  7. Run at 100 V for 30–45 min; monitor dye front

Skill IV — DNA Gel Interpretation (15 min)
  1. Identify the DNA ladder lane; match bands to known size markers (bp)
  2. Estimate sizes of unknown bands by comparing migration distance to ladder
  3. Create a standard curve: log (size) vs. distance migrated
  4. Determine if restriction digest is complete: all expected bands present, no uncut DNA
  5. Report fragment sizes to nearest 100 bp
Skill V — Bradford Protein Quantitative Assay (20 min)
  1. Prepare BSA standards at known concentrations (0.1–1.0 mg/mL)
  2. Add Bradford reagent to each standard and unknown sample
  3. Mix; incubate 5 minutes at room temperature
  4. Measure absorbance at 595 nm using spectrophotometer
  5. Plot standard curve (A595 vs. concentration)
  6. Determine unknown protein concentration from curve
Skill VI — Bacterial Transformation (20 min)
  1. Chill competent cells on ice; thaw gently
  2. Add plasmid DNA (1–5 µL); mix gently — do not vortex
  3. Incubate on ice for 30 minutes
  4. Heat shock at 42°C for exactly 45 seconds
  5. Return to ice immediately for 2 minutes
  6. Add 1 mL SOC or LB media; incubate at 37°C for 60 min with agitation

Written Exam

Structured Debate
Teams are assigned Affirmative or Negative. Each team has 20 minutes of prep time, then presents constructive speeches (3 min each), rebuttals (2 min each), and closing statements (1 min each). Judges score on argument quality, evidence, rebuttal, and delivery.

🏆 What Judges Look For

📚
Scientific Knowledge — accuracy of facts, use of peer-reviewed evidence, correct biomedical terminology
🧠
Argumentation — logical structure, claim → evidence → warrant format, anticipating counterarguments
⚔️
Rebuttal Quality — directly addressing opponent's points, not just restating your case
🎤
Delivery — clarity, pace, eye contact, confidence, professional presence
🤝
Teamwork — coordinated transitions, building on partner's arguments, consistent messaging
⏱️
Time Management — staying within time limits; going over or under costs points

💡 Prep Tips

  4. Drill your rebuttal. Most teams prepare constructive speeches but neglect rebuttal. Write out every major argument the other side could make and your exact counter for each.
  5. Coordinate with your partner. Decide who covers which arguments, practice your transitions, and agree on one consistent framing of your position so judges don't hear conflicting messages.

  1. Spend the first 5 minutes of prep skimming for the strongest data point. One powerful statistic or study will anchor your entire speech. Find it first, then build your argument around it.
  2. Structure every speech the same way before competition day. Hook → Position → Argument 1 → Argument 2 → Concede + Rebut → Call to Action. Practise this structure on different health topics so it's automatic by competition day.
  3. Prepare for the counterargument — pick it apart specifically. Judges reward speakers who say "Opponents argue X — but this misses Y because of Z" rather than just ignoring the other side. Acknowledge the steelman version of the counterargument, then dismantle it.
  4. Know common Canadian health policy contexts cold. Tobacco regulation, cannabis policy, universal pharmacare, vaccine mandates, and mental health funding debates are perennial persuasive speaking topics. Having background knowledge before you see the packet saves critical prep time.
  5. For Q&A, listen carefully and answer the actual question. The most common mistake is deflecting to a pre-prepared talking point. Judges notice. Answer directly first, then expand.
  6. Practise on a 5-minute timer weekly. Five minutes is both shorter and longer than it feels. Record yourself. Cut anything that doesn't serve the argument. Practise until your pacing feels comfortable without a script.

📋 Overview

Teams conduct original health science research — a proper study with a hypothesis, methodology, data collection, analysis, and conclusion — and present findings on a standardised research poster. This is the most academically rigorous HOSA event. Round 1 is a written exam on research methodology; Round 2 is a poster session where judges walk up to your display and ask you to present and defend your research. Teams that have conducted real original research (even small-scale surveys or lab work) have a significant advantage.

🗓 Format & Rounds

Round 1
Written Examination
50 MCQs on research methodology: study design types, hypothesis development, sampling, data collection, statistical analysis basics (mean, standard deviation, p-value, confidence intervals), research ethics (REB/IRB), APA citation, and interpreting results. 45 minutes.
Round 2
Poster Session + Q&A
Display your research poster at an assigned station. Judges rotate through; you deliver a 5–8 minute summary of your research and answer 5–10 minutes of detailed questions. Judges may include university researchers or clinicians with methodological expertise.

🏆 What Judges Look For

🔬
Research Question & Hypothesis — PICO-formatted question, testable hypothesis, clearly defined variables
🗂️
Methodology — appropriate study design for the question; sampling strategy; data collection validity
📈
Analysis & Results — correct statistical approach; accurate presentation of findings; appropriate tables/graphs
💬
Discussion & Conclusion — interpreting results in context of existing literature; acknowledging limitations
🖼️
Poster Design — clear visual hierarchy, readable fonts, logical flow, appropriate use of figures
🎓
Research Defence — explaining methodological choices, defending conclusions, acknowledging limitations honestly

💡 Prep Tips

  1. Conduct real original research — even a simple survey counts. A team that surveyed 80 students at their school about sleep habits and correlated it with academic performance is doing original research. It doesn't need to be a lab study. IRB/REB ethics approval strengthens your project significantly.
  2. Use PICO format to write your research question. Population, Intervention/Exposure, Comparison, Outcome. "In (P) high school students, does (I) daily moderate exercise, compared to (C) sedentary behaviour, reduce (O) self-reported anxiety scores?" is a proper research question.
  3. Know the difference between correlation and causation — and say so in your discussion. Judges will test this. If your study design is observational, you cannot claim causation, and trying to will immediately undermine your credibility.
  4. Study your poster as much as your project. The poster should tell the complete story without you speaking: Introduction → Methods → Results → Discussion → Conclusion. Each section should be scannable in 30 seconds.
  5. Prepare for "what are the limitations of your study?" Every research project has limitations (small sample, convenience sampling, self-report bias, lack of control group). Know yours and say them confidently before judges point them out.
  6. For the written exam, focus on study design and statistics. MCQs heavily test: RCT vs. cohort vs. case-control, Type I vs. Type II error, what a p-value means, how to interpret a confidence interval, and research ethics principles (Belmont Report, autonomy, beneficence, justice).
